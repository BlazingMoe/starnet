WHAT IT IS

A living simulation on your machine where every sprite is a real agent doing real work. Recruit a crew, connect real tools and real models, and build the station into a workforce shaped around you — the only limit is what you ask for.

  • Placed gear is real capability. A signal dish grants web, a cabinet grants files, a workbench grants a terminal — place one and the whole crew draws on it.
  • Every bay is another agent, running its own work in parallel.
  • The floor is honest. An agent at a lit desk means a live run — the timer on screen is the real one.

You direct from COMMS. They think, browse, build files, and deliver — with your models, your keys, on your machine.

03

FEATURES

  • ◉

    IT FINDS WORK BEFORE YOU ASSIGN IT

    Your crew keeps a dossier on its commander — goals, taste, strengths — studies what you ship, and comes back with proposals: new specialists, new missions, new beliefs about what you need. Everything is propose-and-confirm; nothing joins the station without your yes.

  • ▤

    WORKFLOWS YOU BUILD WITH YOUR HANDS

    Lay belts, filters, splitters, joiners, and loops across the floor and the station compiles them into the routing plan the server actually executes. The box on the belt and the agent that picks it up are provably the same plan.

  • ▲

    LEVEL UP BY SHIPPING YOUR LIFE

    Tell the station your real goals and it breaks them into milestone quests advanced only by real completed work. Rate a finished run nailed it and XP mints from your verdict — a station level that records what actually shipped. Meters describe, never gate: full power from minute one.

  • ◆

    A RECOMMENDER WITH A SPINE

    One suggestion at a time, and only with evidence — a recommendation that can't say why is dropped. It ranks 101 built-in mission recipes and every class against your real work history, and drafts a bespoke specialist when nothing on the shelf fits.

  • ◈

    EVERY AGENT REMEMBERS

    Each crew member keeps its own long-term notebook across runs. You keep, edit, or discard every memory before it sticks; trust grows when a memory proves useful and halves every 30 days it goes unused.

  • ☻

    A CREW THAT'S ACTUALLY YOURS

    35 curated classes plus a 20-deep specialist archive, custom classes, per-agent personalities, 36 skins — and a full build mode for the station itself: rooms, floors, walls, and a prop catalog where placed gear is real capability.

  • ↻

    WORK SHARPENS ITSELF

    Rate a delivery and the next pass is built from what you valued. Routines compound instead of decaying.

  • ☾

    NIGHT SHIFT

    The station keeps working while you're away — inside a leash you set (jobs per day, how far an action may reach), sandboxed by default, with an honest morning report and a decision ledger that records every overnight action and the reason it fired.

  • ▣

    IT DRIVES A REAL BROWSER

    Agents don't guess from a search snippet — they navigate, read, click, and type on the live page, and report what the page actually said.

  • ✉

    REACH IT FROM ANYWHERE

    Pair Telegram, Discord, Slack, Matrix, or Signal and your station reports in and takes orders while you're out. One E-STOP — on screen or Alt+H — halts every live run everywhere, and every waiting chat is told so.

  • ♫

    TALK TO IT

    Hands-free Live Voice runs on a speech engine built into the app — no key, no account, and your voice never leaves the machine. Identical on every provider, because it doesn't use one.

  • ❖

    AGENTS SUMMON AGENTS

    Give one agent a big job and it can dispatch subtasks to crew it summons, then fold the results back — you watch the whole tree run on the floor.

  • ■

    NOTHING FAKE, EVER

    The app never shows state the harness can't prove. No fake progress, no invented counters, real costs — an append-only ledger records every run's true spend.

  • ⚿

    LOCAL-FIRST, YOUR KEYS

    Everything stays on your machine. 18 providers, three no-key OAuth sign-ins, a free local option, or StarNet credits. Zero telemetry — we collect nothing.

  • ⇆

    PLAYS WELL WITH OTHER HARNESSES

    An MCP bridge and OpenAI-style endpoint let Claude Code, Hermes, or OpenClaw drive your station. Coming from one of those?

FAQ

Is StarNet free?

The software is MIT-licensed open source and free to run. Model usage costs whatever your provider charges — you bring your own API key (or OAuth account), and StarNet shows you real cost per run. No markup, no subscription required to use your own keys. If you'd rather not manage keys at all, StarNet Credits are an optional managed plan — never required.

Is the work actually real?

Yes — that's the whole point. Every agent run is a real model call with real tools; files get written, browsers get driven, and the ledger records real cost. The station never renders state the harness can't prove. If it looks like an agent is working, it is.

Do I have to build conveyor belts to use it?

No. Chatting with an agent from COMMS needs no belts at all. Belts are for real jobs — work with several parts, or work that should flow through several agents on its own, especially while you're away. The conveyor guide shows the whole idea in five minutes, and the LINES tool drops a finished line in one click.

Where does my data live?

On your machine. Station state, transcripts, memory, and ledgers live in your local StarNet workspace. Requests go to your model provider only when an agent runs, and to the wider network only when you explicitly grant a network tool or connector.

What do I need to start?

A Windows or macOS machine and one model provider: an OpenRouter API key works everywhere, a ChatGPT, Grok, or Kimi sign-in needs no key at all, and Ollama runs free and fully local. That's it — full power from minute one, nothing gated behind progression.

Can it spend money without me noticing?

No. Every conveyor line has a per-job budget ($2.00 unless you raise it, $50 ceiling) and an optional per-day cap; the BUDGET panel adds hard per-run, per-agent, per-day, and global caps the sidecar enforces against the real ledger. Night Shift is limited to a number of unattended jobs per day you choose, and the E-STOP halts everything at once.

Why does my OS warn me during install?

SmartScreen can still warn while an Authenticode certificate builds reputation; inspect the publisher before proceeding. A public Mac DMG is required to pass Apple notarization, so a Gatekeeper trust failure should be reported rather than bypassed. The troubleshooting guide explains both cases.
