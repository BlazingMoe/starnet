HOW CONVEYORS WORK

Belts are how you tell your agents what order to work in.

PLAIN ENGLISH~5 MINNO SETUP NEEDED TO READ

01The whole idea

Say you want your station to answer a question. One agent looks things up. A second one writes the answer. A belt is just you drawing the order: this one first, then that one.

The box sliding along the belt is the actual job. Nothing is pretend — if a box is moving, an agent is really working right now. If the floor is still, nothing is running.

A BOXone job
A DESKone agent
A BELTthe order they work in
A LINEthe whole thing, start to finish

02Why bother?

Two agents beat one

One agent doing research and writing does both jobs half as well. A belt lets you give each part to someone good at it.

It runs without you

A message that arrives while you’re asleep follows the belt on its own. You wake up to the answer, not the question.

You can see it happening

No hidden queue, no spinner. The box on the belt is the job. You can watch it move.

03The four pieces

Open REFIT STATION to build. Everything below is under PROP 6, and the belt itself is BELT 7.

THE FRONT DOOR
called an INBOX
Work from outside — a text you send, a job that runs on a timer — lands here and steps onto the belt.
A DESK
called a BAY
One agent sits here. A box arriving at their desk is their next job.
THE OUT TRAY
called an OUTBOX
Where the finished answer leaves. Click it to read everything that’s come back.
A SIGNPOST
FILTER · SPLITTER · JOINER · LOOP
Small tiles that sit on the belt and decide which way a box goes next.
// YOU CAN SKIP THE FRONT DOORA desk on its own is enough for an agent to work, and anything you type in chat goes straight to them. The front door is only for work arriving from outside.

04Build one

  1. Put down the pieces

    Hit REFIT STATION, take PROP 6, and place a front door, a desk and an out tray in a row. Leave a few empty squares between them — that’s where the belt goes.

    machines down, gaps left for belt
  2. Join them up

    Take BELT 7 and click one, then the next. The belt draws itself, corners and all.

    BELT tool: two clicks per link
  3. Sit an agent at the desk

    Click the desk and pick an agent. The belt lights up the moment someone’s sitting there — that glow is the app telling you the wiring is right.

    a bound bay energizes the run
  4. Say what it’s allowed to spend

    Click the front door to name the line and cap its spending. Leave it blank and you get the defaults: 6 steps and $2.00 per message.

    the INBOX card holds the caps
  5. Send it something

    Leave build mode and give it a real job. A box slides out of the front door, reaches the desk, and drops in. That agent is now working.

    a real crate = a real run
// OR SKIP ALL FIVE STEPSThe LINES tool 9 drops a whole finished line in one click. See Your First Working Line.

05Is it working?

Glowing means working. Dark means something’s missing.

You never have to guess. The app re-checks your belts every time you change them and shows you the answer on the floor itself.

GLOWING

The belt reaches a desk with someone at it. Arrows march, the light blinks, boxes ride.

DARK

Same belt, empty desk at the end. Arrows frozen, light out, the whole run dimmed.

// THE ONE EVERYONE HITSA belt that only touches the corner of a signpost tile feeds nothing. It has to run through the tile — in one side, out the other.

06When a straight line isn’t enough

Four shapes cover almost everything. Each one is just belts and a signpost tile.

ONE AFTER ANOTHERHAND-OFF

The first agent’s answer becomes the second agent’s job. Agent A looks it up, agent B writes it up, and what comes back to you is B’s version.

THIS WAY OR THAT WAYFILTER

The signpost reads each job and picks a lane — code to the engineer, everything else to whoever handles the rest.

ALL AT ONCESPLITTER

One stream spread across three agents working at the same time. Three boxes in, three jobs started — nothing quietly disappears.

ROUND AGAINLOOP

Write it, criticise it, rewrite it. The one safe way to go in a circle: five laps by default, and it still can’t spend more than the line’s cap.

// THE EXPENSIVE MISTAKETwo desks that feed each other would bounce work back and forth forever, and you’d pay for every lap. The app refuses to run it and tells you which handoff to cut.
// A BROKEN STEP NEVER EATS YOUR ANSWERCap reached, step failed, you hit stop — you still get the last good answer, plus a note saying where it stopped.

07Something’s not running

The app writes the problem on the machine itself, in words, before you’re charged anything. These four are almost always it:

NO AGENT — CLICK

Nobody’s sitting at this desk. Click it and pick an agent.

NOT ON THE LINE

No belt is touching this desk at all. It’s just furniture until you join it up.

NOT FED

There are belts, but none of them actually reach this desk. Usually a belt clipping the corner of a signpost tile instead of running through it.

NOT CONNECTED

The front door has no belt leaving it. Take BELT 7, click the door, then click a desk.

The other 8 messages, and what each one means
DUP AGENT — ONE BAY EACH

The same agent is sitting at two desks. One desk each.

A PROP SITS ON THIS LINE — MOVE IT

You put furniture on top of a belt square. Move the furniture, not the belt.

SPLITTER NEEDS 2 OUT-LANES

A splitter with one way out is just a belt square. Run belts through it: in one side, out two.

JOINER NEEDS 2 IN-LANES

A joiner waits for several branches to arrive. With one branch there’s nothing to wait for.

NO DEFAULT LANE — CLICK

A filter with no fallback. Nothing gets lost — it takes the first lane — but set the default so you choose.

LOOP! — BREAK THE CIRCLE

Your belts form a ring. A box would go round forever. Add a LOOP tile, or delete a square.

WORK LINE LOOPS — CUT ONE HANDOFF

No ring in the belts, but two desks feed each other — work would bounce between them forever, and you’d pay for every lap.

LOOP HAS NO DONE / BACK LANE

A LOOP tile needs both exits: one carrying finished work onward, one going back to an earlier desk.

// NOTHING YOU DO HERE IS PERMANENTDELETE 5 removes anything — drag it along a belt to clear the whole run. Every change can be undone, so poke at it.

08Questions people actually ask

Do I even need belts?

No. An agent sitting at a desk works fine on its own, and anything you type in chat goes straight to them. Belts are for work arriving from outside, and for handing one agent’s answer to the next.

My belt is dark. Why?

Because it doesn’t finish anywhere useful. Follow it to the end — you’ll find an empty desk, a gap, or a belt clipping the corner of a signpost instead of going through it.

Can two belts feed the same desk?

Yes. Boxes queue up rather than collide. What you can’t do is sit one agent at two desks.

Could a long chain run up a big bill?

Each line has a cap on how many steps it takes and how much it can spend — 6 steps and $2.00 per message unless you change it. When it hits the cap it stops, hands you what it has, and tells you it stopped.

Does it keep going when I close the window?

The work does. The box is a picture of a real job — the job runs whether or not you’re watching, and the floor catches up when you look.
